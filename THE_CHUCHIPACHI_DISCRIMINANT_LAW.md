# The Chuchipachi Discriminant Law
### Discriminant laws for the linear Hodge lattices of Fermat surfaces: one exponent formula for every degree, five blind verdicts, and an off-by-one in a published table

**Author:** Rafael Amichis Luengo (Madrid) · tretoef@gmail.com · github.com/tretoef-estrella
**Date:** 4 June 2026 · **Status of the central law:** [CONJ, blind-tested] — 12/12 in-sample, 5/5 blind cells, priority swept (§5)
**Hardware:** every computation in this note runs in seconds on one thread of a consumer laptop.

## 0. The story in one paragraph

In 1987, trying to understand a square that kept appearing in norms of Jacobi sums, Shioda conjectured that the Néron–Severi lattice of the complex Fermat surface of **prime** degree m has discriminant exactly m^{3(m−3)²}. In 2010, Schütt, Shioda and van Luijk verified the determinant for every **odd** degree up to 81 — their basis machinery requires ω = −1, which exists only for odd m — and there the formula stopped: the even half of the family had no law, stated or verified, anywhere. In 2017, Aljovin, Movasati and Villaflor published the elementary divisors of the lattices of linear Hodge cycles for degrees 3 through 14, computed by brute Smith normal form — and, read with fresh eyes, their twelve rows contain the missing half. This note states the unified law for **all** degrees, reports five sealed blind predictions verified exactly — three of them in even degree, territory no published source covers — together with a finer law for the divisor *profiles*, a corner law for when the lattice glues maximally to its transcendental complement, and one off-by-one in the published table, caught by an independently measured rank.

## 1. The objects

Let S = X²_m ⊂ P³ be the complex Fermat surface of degree m and let V = V²_m be the lattice of **linear Hodge cycles** of Aljovin–Movasati–Villaflor (AMV) [3] — the saturated sublattice of H²(S, Z) cut out by the integral vanishing conditions of their §3, rationally generated by the classes of the 3m² lines (Aoki, Theorem A; for m ≤ 4 or gcd(m,6) = 1 it is all of NS(S)). AMV's Table 1 lists the elementary divisors and rank of V for 3 ≤ m ≤ 14; their Theorem 1 states that the line classes generate V integrally for every row of that table. All lattice data in this note live in V's **full register** (Table 1 of [3]); conversions to the primitive register use AMV's stated rule.

## 2. The unified law

> **Law (the Chuchipachi discriminant law) [CONJ, blind-tested].** For the Fermat surface of degree m ≥ 3,
>
> |disc V(2,m)| = m^{3(m−3)²} for m odd, and |disc V(2,m)| = m^{3(m−3)²} · (m/4)³ for m even.

Verified against all twelve rows of AMV Table 1 (m = 3, …, 14), prime powers included with no further correction: m = 4 gives 2⁶ (the row 8²), m = 8 gives 2²²⁸, m = 9 gives 3²¹⁶, m = 12 gives 2⁴⁸⁶·3²⁴⁶. For m = 2q with q an odd prime the factor (m/4)³ = (q/2)³ splits as **−3 on the prime 2 and +3 on q**: exp₂ = 12(q−1)(q−2) and exp_q = exp₂ + 6, which is how the law first surfaced — as two separate laws with a mysterious constant gap of 6, later recognized as the two faces of one cube. We refer to the statement above as the **Chuchipachi law** — a house name, in the repository's tradition that a playful name must carry a serious result.

## 3. The profile law — finer than the determinant

The runs return the full elementary-divisor profiles, not just determinants. For the odd prime cells the heights never exceed 2, and the counts obey a law of their own:

> **Profile law [CONJ].** For odd prime m ≥ 7: #(m²-divisors) = 3m − 16 and #(m-divisors) = 3m² − 24m + 59; hence profile = m^{3m²−24m+59} · (m²)^{3m−16}.

Five of five on m = 7, 11, 13, 17, 19 (the last two computed here for the first time: 17⁵¹⁸·289³⁵ and 19⁶⁸⁶·361⁴¹). The exception is named, not hidden: at m = 5 the height-2 count is 1, not 3·5−16 = −1; the consistency checksum h₁ + 2h₂ = 3(m−3)² nevertheless holds at all six points including the exception. No swept source publishes these profiles — [2] published determinants only, and [3] stop at m = 14. The raw even-degree profiles from the blind runs, published nowhere, are recorded in the repository run logs (e.g. (2,16) at p = 2: heights {2:12, 4:432, 5:2, 6:8, 8:28}); they are logged raw, deliberately unfished.

## 4. Two corner observations

**The ceiling corner [FIRM on 19 clean published points].** Write ℓ for the total number of nontrivial elementary divisors of V and T for the transcendental lattice. Since V is saturated in the unimodular H²(S, Z) (and the analogous statement holds for the fourfold lattices of AMV's tables), disc(V) ≅ disc(T) and ℓ ≤ min(rank V, rank T). Across AMV's sixteen Table 1 rows **and** the three uncontaminated rows of their Table 2 (converted by their full↔primitive rule, the conversion validated by reconstructing the (4,4) full row byte-exact): **ℓ = rank T exactly iff n ≤ 4 and d ≤ 4** — the four cells (2,3), (2,4), (4,3), (4,4) — with strict slack everywhere else. A first guess, "ceiling iff d ≤ 4", is refuted by the clean d = 3 column itself: the slack at (6,3), (8,3), (10,3) is 1, 11, 78 — a gradient that grows with the dimension and is recorded as an open structure question. Cyclotomic-field explanations die with the first guess: those three cells live in the same Q(ζ₃) as the ceiling cells.

**The erratum [FIRM].** The (6,4) row of AMV's Table 2 prints −1⁵⁷⁶·2⁶⁸·4⁴⁹·8²⁹⁶·16⁴⁸·32⁶⁹·512², whose multiplicities sum to **1108**. The rank of that lattice is **1107**: it equals DS₆(4) of Degtyarev–Shimada [4, Remark 4.4], a value this campaign has measured independently on hardware, and which is also the central trinomial walk count T(8) = 1107. The row-sum checksum is validated 3-of-3 on the table's other rows — (6,3) sums to 70 = DS₆(3), (8,3) to 252 = DS₈(3), (10,3) to 924 = the campaign's own measured closing rank of (10,3) — so exactly one printed exponent in the (6,4) row is off by one. Identifying which requires the authors' raw data. The table's mathematical content is otherwise confirmed by every check this note runs; the misprint affects no theorem of [3].

Two registers protect the even half twice over: Shioda's question concerns NS, and for even m ≥ 6 the lines do not rationally generate NS [2, Thm 3.1] — the even law lives on AMV's V, an object the 1987 question does not touch.

**The route to a theorem.** Shioda's own paper contains the anatomy of the odd-prime law: each transcendental Galois orbit contributes a norm B·m³/p^w with B a square (the nondegenerate pairing on the Brauer group, his Prop 6.2), there are exactly (m−3)² such orbits (his (7.7)), and the refined Artin–Tate formula (his (7.5)) stacks them into m^{3(m−3)²}. The prime-only ingredient is Iwasawa's congruence j(α) ≡ 1 mod (1−ζ_m)³; the even correction (m/4)³ = m³/2⁶ is a candidate 2-adic defect of exactly that congruence. An even-degree analogue of his §3 combined with his §6 is the pencil route from [CONJ] to theorem.

## 6. The instrument

GRAM_MANOMETER_SURFACE_v4.py (repository): builds the Gram matrix of the 3m² lines from the intersection rules (5) of [2] — self-intersection 2 − m, and for even m the (1,3) rule carries the factor ω² explicitly (with γ := ω² a primitive m-th root, the condition reads k′ ≡ k + l + l′ + 1) — then extracts the p-adic elementary divisors by layered elimination with a shrinking working modulus. **Gates before every shot, byte-exact against AMV Table 1:** (2,5) rank 37, profile 5¹⁰·25; (2,7) rank 91, 7³⁸·49⁵; (2,6) rank 62, exp₂ 24 / exp₃ 30; (2,8) rank 128, exp₂ 228. For odd m the general rule reproduces the ω = −1 instrument exactly, so the odd gates double as an invariance test. One self-caught bug is part of the record: the v2 elimination kept reducing modulo p^K after layer descents, leaving stale digits above the valid precision; v3 fixed it by shrinking the modulus with each descent, and the gates were re-passed before any sealed shot.

**Register caveat, stated because it is load-bearing.** For even m the lines generate V — but "lines generate V" is AMV's Theorem 1, proven for m ≤ 14. At m = 16, 18, 20 the blind PASSes are therefore **double evidence**: the exponent law and the integral generation both extend. A failure by an even excess would have required an index² analysis before any conclusion; none occurred.

## 7. What is explicitly not claimed

No progress on any Hodge-type conjecture. The lattices here are torsion-free; the laws describe their discriminant structure, a different invariant from the torsion questions of the parent campaign, whose state is unchanged. The unified law is a conjecture with a 17-point record (12 in-sample, 5 blind); the profile law a conjecture with 5 points and one named exception; the ceiling corner a verified finite pattern; the erratum a checksum fact. Each claim carries its flag, and the verifier scripts in the repository recompute every number in this note from the published tables and the raw run logs.
