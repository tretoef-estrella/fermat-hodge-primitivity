## Statement

Let S = F₃[s₀,…,s₅]. For each of the fifteen perfect matchings J of {0,…,5} let I_J = (s_a+s_b : {a,b} ∈ J), and let E = (e₁, e₃, e₅) be the odd symmetric ideal, which by the Odd Symmetric Theorem (standalone, on disk) equals ∩_J I_J. For v ≥ 1 write Q = 3^v and m_Q = (s₀^Q,…,s₅^Q).

> **Descent Theorem.** For every v ≥ 1:  ∩_J (I_J + m_Q) = (e₁, e₃, e₅) + m_Q.

> **Descent Principle (general form of the inductive step).** Let {I_α} be any finite family of ideals of F_p[s₁,…,s_n] generated by linear forms, and E := ∩_α I_α. If ∩_α(I_α + m_p) = E + m_p, then ∩_α(I_α + m_{p^v}) = E + m_{p^v} for every v ≥ 1. (Below we write the case p = 3, n = 6; the proof is verbatim general.)

The Descent Theorem is the statement (★₃₄) of the campaign — the bone — at every level of the Fermat tower; through the campaign's sealed sufficiency chain (§244–§245, §239, §242, §73.3 of the master; THE_NAIL §8.5) it yields Tors₃(coker Δ_v) = 0 for all v, i.e. Degtyarev–Shimada Conjecture 1.2 in dimension 4.

## Preliminaries

F : S → S, a ↦ a³ is the Frobenius; F(S) = F₃[s₀³,…,s₅³]. Four standard facts, each with a short proof:

**(L2)** Cubing is F₃-linear on forms: (Σaᵢsᵢ)³ = Σaᵢsᵢ³ since aᵢ³ = aᵢ. Iterating, φ^Q ∈ m_Q for every form φ, and for **any frame** (F₃-basis of forms) y₁,…,y₆: m_Q = (y₁^Q,…,y₆^Q) — invert the linear map on Q-th powers.

**(K)** S is a **free F(S)-module** with basis the 3⁶ reduced monomials (all exponents ≤ 2) of any frame: base-3 digit expansion of exponents gives existence and uniqueness; F(S) = F₃[y₁³,…,y₆³] in every frame by (L2). Every t ∈ S therefore has a unique per-frame **digit expansion** t = Σ_ρ F(t_ρ)·ρ.

**(F-stability)** Every ideal contains the cubes of its elements: f ∈ I ⟹ f³ = f·f² ∈ I.

**(m^{[3]})** If w ∈ m_{Q′} then w³ ∈ m_Q: write w = Σhᵢsᵢ^{Q′}; then w³ = Σhᵢ³sᵢ^{3Q′} by the freshman's dream.

## Proof

**Lemma A (digit transport across frames).** Between any two frames the digit vectors of an element are related by fixed S-linear matrices. *Proof.* Write each reduced monomial μ of the first frame in the second frame's digit basis: μ = Σ_ρ F(a_{μρ})ρ, a_{μρ} ∈ S, a fixed finite matrix. Then t = Σ_μ F(t_μ)μ = Σ_ρ F(Σ_μ t_μ a_{μρ})ρ, using that F is additive and multiplicative in characteristic 3; uniqueness of the ρ-expansion gives t^{(y)}_ρ = Σ_μ a_{μρ} t_μ. The inverse matrix exists symmetrically. ∎

**Lemma B (relative digit descent, per leg).** Fix a matching J and a frame y whose first three coordinates are the leg forms. Then I_J + m_Q = (y₁, y₂, y₃, y₄^Q, y₅^Q, y₆^Q) is a **monomial ideal** of the frame (m_Q = (y_j^Q) by L2), and, with Q = 3Q′:

> t ∈ I_J + m_Q  ⟺  every J-frame digit t^{(J)}_ρ ∈ I_J + m_{Q′}.

*Proof.* Membership in a monomial ideal is checked monomial-by-monomial, and the y-digit classes partition the y-monomials by their exponent residues mod 3, so the condition splits over the digits. For a reduced ρ, a monomial of F(t_ρ)ρ is divisible by yᵢ (i ≤ 3) iff the corresponding monomial of t_ρ is; and divisible by y_j^Q (j ≥ 4) iff 3e + ρ_j ≥ Q where e is the corresponding exponent of t_ρ and ρ_j ≤ 2 — **iff e ≥ Q′, with an exact threshold**: e = Q′−1 gives 3Q′−3+ρ_j < Q, while e = Q′ gives ≥ Q. Hence F(t_ρ)ρ lies in the ideal iff every monomial of t_ρ lies in (y₁,y₂,y₃) + (y₄^{Q′}, y₅^{Q′}, y₆^{Q′}) = I_J + m_{Q′}. ∎

**Lemma C (frame independence).** [∀ρ: t^{(J)}_ρ ∈ I_J + m_{Q′}] ⟺ [∀μ: t_μ ∈ I_J + m_{Q′}], where t_μ are the digits in one fixed standard frame. *Proof.* I_J + m_{Q′} is an ideal; by Lemma A each family of digits consists of S-linear combinations of the other, and ideals absorb S-combinations. ∎

**Inductive step.** Let t ∈ ∩_J(I_J + m_Q), Q = 3Q′. For each J, Lemma B places the J-frame digits of t in I_J + m_{Q′}, and Lemma C converts this to the **standard** digits: t_μ ∈ I_J + m_{Q′} for every μ. Since this holds for every J *simultaneously* — the same fixed digits t_μ — we get

> t_μ ∈ ∩_J (I_J + m_{Q′}) = E + m_{Q′}

by the inductive hypothesis. Note the hypothesis is applied to the **full fifteen-fold intersection, whole, at every rung**: the argument never factors through a single leg (the per-leg lemmas only bookkeep digits; the global cancellation travels inside the ∩). Write t_μ = ε_μ + w_μ with ε_μ ∈ E, w_μ ∈ m_{Q′}, and reassemble:

> t = Σ_μ F(ε_μ)μ + Σ_μ F(w_μ)μ.

The first sum lies in E by (F-stability); the second lies in m_Q by (m^{[3]}). Hence t ∈ E + m_Q. The same three lemmas prove the general Descent Principle verbatim, since nothing beyond linearity of the generators was used. ∎ (step)
