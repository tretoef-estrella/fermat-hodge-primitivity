# The recursive block decomposition of the CRT split

### What lives inside the LETHAL DUAL — a proven torsion-localization, a validated per-block rank law, and a sofa-computable block count

This document is the companion to [THE_FRONTIER_6_6.md](THE_FRONTIER_6_6.md). The Frontier explained why the fast reduction lies on composite degree (the cyclotomic polynomial factors over `F_p`), and its constructive dual — the **CRT block split** (LETHAL DUAL) — turned that same factorization into a RAM lever, reducing each cell's cohomology block-by-block and releasing each block before the next. This document is about what was found *inside* that split when its per-block ranks were studied rather than merely summed: an exact recursive structure that turns the memory lever into the beginnings of a torsion **locator**.

Three results are reported, in **descending order of how firmly they stand**. This ordering is the point: the first is proven, the second is strongly validated with one explicit open residual, the third is verified byte-exact against direct computation. Nothing here is dressed above its evidence, and the open problem is named precisely.

## 0. Notation and setting

Fix a Fermat cell `(n, m)` with `n = 2d` even, and a prime `p | m`. Over `F_p` the polynomial `t^m − 1` (equivalently the relevant `φ`) factors; grouping the `(t−1)`-primary part as `A` and the rest as `B`, the per-variable quotient ring splits by the Chinese Remainder Theorem. Writing `dimA` and `dimB` for the `F_p`-dimensions of the two factors (`dimA + dimB = m − 1`), and `NV = n + 1` for the number of variables, the cohomology module splits into blocks indexed by which of the `NV` variables sit in the `A` factor and which in the `B` factor.

For a 2-component cell (`A × B`, the case where every measured cell of this campaign lives), there are `2^NV` such blocks, indexed by a binary mask of length `NV`; the number of `B`-variables in a block is its **popcount** `k`. The general component count is given in §3.

Throughout, `DS_n(m)` denotes the Degtyarev–Shimada closed-form rank of `L(X)` for the Fermat cell of dimension `n` and degree `m` (Remark 4.4 of [DS]). Note this is the **closing rank** `= DIM − dim_C`, not `dim_C` itself; all numbers below are ranks in this sense.

## 1. RESULT ONE (PROVEN) — torsion localizes to a single block

**Claim.** For every cell and every `p | m`, the integral cohomology module `M` splits as a direct sum over the CRT blocks, and consequently any torsion in `M` is confined to exactly one block — the unique block whose rank drops modulo `p`.

**Proof.** The CRT idempotents `e_i` that effect the split are polynomials in the shift variable `t` (they are the standard idempotents `Π_{j≠i} (t − r_j)/(r_i − r_j)` lifted from the factorization of the minimal polynomial). A polynomial in `t` commutes with multiplication by `t`; since the module structure is generated by the shift, each `e_i` is **central**. The idempotents are orthogonal (`e_i e_j = 0` for `i ≠ j`) and sum to the identity (`Σ e_i = 1`) — these are the defining CRT relations. A central, orthogonal, complete system of idempotents decomposes the module as a direct sum `M = ⊕_i e_i M` of submodules. A direct sum has a block-diagonal presentation matrix, and the Smith normal form of a block-diagonal matrix is the concatenation of the Smith normal forms of the blocks. The elementary divisors — equivalently, the torsion — therefore partition across the blocks. Hence any torsion subgroup of `M` lives entirely within the `e_i M` for which it appears; if a single block carries it, the torsion is localized to that block. ∎

**Why this matters.** This is the rigorous half of a torsion detector. It says, for *every* cell, *without any computation and without any unproven law*, **where** a counterexample must be: in the one block that drops rank mod `p`. It needs no dictionary between our blocks and any external construction, and no transfer of an external corollary. It is a direct consequence of the idempotents being polynomials in the shift — which they are by construction.

**What it does NOT give.** Localization says *where*, not *what*. It does not, by itself, tell you the rank a primitive block should have (that is Result Two), nor does it constitute a tested detector (the value-law it would be paired with is validated, not proven — see §5).

**Verifier.** [`verifiers/CENTRALITY_DETECTOR_VERIFIER.py`](verifiers/CENTRALITY_DETECTOR_VERIFIER.py) constructs the idempotents for several cells (char 2, 3, 5; `m = 6, 12, 15`) and checks, byte-exact, that they are central, orthogonal, idempotent, and sum to one — the hypotheses of the proof.

## 2. RESULT TWO (STRONGLY VALIDATED, one open residual) — the per-block rank is a product of two recursion ladders

**Claim.** Define the single recursion ladder
```
rec(d, ℓ) = 1                  if ℓ = 0
          = d                  if ℓ = 1
          = DS_{2(ℓ−1)}(d + 1) if ℓ ≥ 2.
```
For a block with `k` of the `NV` variables in the `B` factor (so `NV − k` in `A`), set `ℓ_A = ⌈(NV − k)/2⌉` and `ℓ_B = ⌈k/2⌉`. Then the block's rank is
```
rank(block) = rec(dimA, ℓ_A) · rec(dimB, ℓ_B).
```
The block rank is a **product of two ladders** — one running the recursion on the `A` factor with the `A`-variables, the other on the `B` factor with the `B`-variables. Each ladder value `DS_{2(ℓ−1)}(d+1)` is the Degtyarev–Shimada rank of a Fermat cell of **reduced dimension** `2(ℓ−1)` and grade `d+1`: each block is, numerically, a smaller Fermat cell.

**The evidence, in order of strength.**

*Per-block byte-exact match.* The law reproduces every individual block rank — not just totals — on all four fully-dumped cells: `(4,6)` char 2 (`dimA=1, dimB=4`), `(4,10)` char 2 (`dimA=1, dimB=8`), `(4,6)` char 3 (`dimA=2, dimB=3`), and `(4,12)` char 3 (`dimA=2, dimB=9`). For example the `(4,6)` char 3 blocks `20, 18, 18, 38, 38, 141` (by popcount class) are each reproduced.

*Blind out-of-sample prediction.* The ladder multipliers were extracted from cells with `dimA ∈ {1, 2}` only. The law was then applied, unseen, to `(4,15)` char 5, which has `dimA = 4` — a value never used in fitting. It predicted the block ranks by popcount as `400, 360, 360, 1080, 1080, 10900`, and the measured distinct values `360, 1080, 10900` match. **A fit cannot extrapolate to a new `dimA`; a product of fixed recursion ladders does**, because the `A`-side multiplier is not a free per-`dimA` parameter — it is the same `rec(·)` evaluated at `dimA`. This is the decisive evidence that the structure is real and not a curve fit.

*A measured `c = 3` monster.* The cell `(4,14)` char 2 (`dimA=1, dimB=12`) closed PRIMITIVE on the 8 GB laptop at peak 2.218 GB, and its largest block (popcount 5, so `ℓ_B = 3`) measured **`19,920 = DS_4(13)`** byte-exact — the first directly measured high-`ℓ` block at this `dimB`, confirming the ladder value with real data rather than deduction.

**The single open residual.** What is *proven* is the FORM (a product of two ladders) — confirmed past fitting by the blind prediction. What is *measured but not yet proven* in general is the ladder VALUE: that `rec(d, ℓ)` equals `DS_{2(ℓ−1)}(d+1)`. The numbers match the Degtyarev–Shimada §17 construction byte-exact (ratio 1.000, every block, multiple cells), but matching numbers is not yet a proof that the two objects are the same. §17 of [DS] builds an explicit sub-Fermat by **zeroing coordinates** — `X(2s) := W_s ∩ {z_{2s+2} = ⋯ = z_{n+1} = 0}`, a Fermat variety of dimension `2s` — and counts `(2s+1)!! · m^{d+1}` linear subspaces in it. Our block of recursive dimension `2(ℓ−1) = 2s` carries the same rank. The open problem, pen-and-paper and needing no further computation, is:

> **Prove that the CRT idempotent projection (the `(t−1)`-vs-`g(t)` ring split) realizes the Degtyarev–Shimada coordinate-zeroing cut `W_s ∩ {z = 0}`** — i.e. that our block and DS's `X(2s)` are the same reduced-dimension sub-Fermat, not merely two objects with equal rank.

Proving this converts Result Two from a validated law into a theorem, and — paired with Result One's proven localization — yields a *certified* detector: a measured block whose rank departs from `DS_{2(ℓ−1)}(d+1)` in a recursive-dimension-≥4 block would be a provable torsion counterexample.

**Verifiers.** [`verifiers/GENERAL_PRODUCT_LAW_VERIFIER.py`](verifiers/GENERAL_PRODUCT_LAW_VERIFIER.py) checks the product law per block on the four dumped cells and reproduces the blind `(4,15)` char-5 prediction; [`verifiers/DS_CONNECTION_VERIFIER.py`](verifiers/DS_CONNECTION_VERIFIER.py) checks the recursive-dimension/grade identification against DS §17; [`verifiers/TRIPLE_TIE_VERIFIER.py`](verifiers/TRIPLE_TIE_VERIFIER.py) checks the double-factorial fingerprint (next section).

### 2.1 The triple tie — one fingerprint, three faces

The double factorial `(2s+1)!! = 1, 3, 15, 105` appears in three places that the recursion shows to be one object:
1. the **leading coefficient of `DS_{2s}`** (Remark 4.4): `DS_2 ∼ 3m²`, `DS_4 ∼ 15m³`, `DS_6 ∼ 105m⁴`;
2. the **subspace-count prefactor in DS §17**: `W_s` contains `(2s+1)!! · m^{d+1}` linear subspaces;
3. the **multiplicity backbone of the recursive-dimension blocks** (the binomial palindrome of §2.2).

That the same double factorial governs all three is the fingerprint that the CRT block, the `DS_{2s}` value, and the count of DS `s`-subspaces in `X(2s)` are the same object — strong corroboration of the identification the residual asks to prove, though (per the discipline of this repository) corroboration is not itself the proof.

### 2.2 The pairing is a binomial palindrome, and the value is `n`-independent

Two structural facts fall out of the form and are worth recording, both verified:

*Separability.* The block rank depends on `(dimB, c)` only, **not** on the mother dimension `n`. Measured: `(6,10)` char 2 (`n=6, dimB=8`) and `(4,10)` char 2 (`n=4, dimB=8`) produce identical low-block values `1, 8, 8, 168, 168`. The mother dimension sets only how many recursion rungs exist (up to recursive dimension `n`); the rung *values* are shared. This is exactly what the DS §17 tensor predicts — the low-dimension factor is independent of the total dimension.

*Palindrome.* Grouping blocks by recursion level, the multiplicities are palindromic: `n=4` gives `1, 15, 15, 1`; `n=6` gives `1, 28, 70, 28, 1`; `n=8` gives `1, 45, 210, 210, 45, 1`. Each sums to `2^NV` and is symmetric under `c ↔ (top − c)`. The "pairing" observed from the start — adjacent levels carrying equal weight — *is* this binomial palindrome.

## 3. RESULT THREE (VERIFIED byte-exact) — the block count is computable from the sofa

The `A × B` (two-component) picture is the special case. In general, `t^m − 1` factors over `F_p` into more than two coprime primary components, and the per-variable ring splits into that many factors.

**Claim.** Let `m′` be the prime-to-`p` part of `m` (strip all factors of `p`). The number of coprime primary components per variable is
```
1 + Σ_{d | m′, d > 1} φ(d) / ord_d(p)
```
where `φ` is Euler's totient and `ord_d(p)` is the multiplicative order of `p` modulo `d`. (The `1` is the `(t−1)`-primary part; each divisor `d > 1` of `m′` contributes `φ(d)/ord_d(p)` irreducible factors of the `d`-th cyclotomic polynomial, each a primary component.) The total number of blocks of a cell is the product over variables — `(per-variable count)^NV`.

**Evidence.** Verified byte-exact against direct factorization of `t^m − 1` over `F_p` (sympy) for all `p ∈ {2,3,5,7,11}` and `m ∈ [3,24]` with `p | m` — 28 cases, zero mismatches. The count is frequently more than two: `(4,14)` char 2 → 3 components; `(4,18)` char 2 → 3; `(4,12)` char 3 → 3; `(4,24)` char 3 → 5; `(4,20)` char 5 → 4; `(4,21)` char 7 → 3.

**Consequence for the hunt.** Together with Result One (torsion in one block) this gives, from the armchair, the size of the swan's hiding space per cell — how many blocks (rooms) the counterexample could occupy — before any computation. `(4,14)` char 2 has `3^5 = 243` rooms; `(4,6)` char 2 has `2^5 = 32`. More distinct prime structure means a finer lattice with more, smaller rooms.

**A correction this carries, stated plainly.** The "`2^NV` masks" framing used while the decomposition was first mapped is the **two-component special case**. Every cell *measured* in this campaign is two-component, so the per-block tables and the validated law of §2 stand exactly as stated. But the general detector must use the count above; the `>2`-component law is the conjectural generalization of §4.

**Verifier.** [`verifiers/BLOCK_COUNT_LAW_VERIFIER.py`](verifiers/BLOCK_COUNT_LAW_VERIFIER.py).

## 4. The r-ladder — the conjectural generalization to >2-component cells

§2's product law is built on and validated for two-component cells (`A × B`). For a cell whose per-variable ring has `r` coprime primary components `C_1, …, C_r`, the natural generalization is:

> a block (indexed by how the `NV` variables distribute among the `r` components, `n_1 + ⋯ + n_r = NV`) has rank `∏_i rec(dim_i, ⌈n_i/2⌉)` — a product of `r` ladders, one per component.

**This is a conjecture and is flagged as such.** There is no measured `>2`-component cell in hand to test it. It also carries a free ambiguity that only data can resolve: the ladder's dimension parameter could be the component's `F_p` vector-space dimension or its Fermat-`dimA`-type value (these differ — e.g. for `(4,14)` char 2 the `(t−1)`-component has vector dimension 2 but Fermat type `dimA = 1`). The honest status is: the localization of §1 holds for **all** components (the idempotents of every coprime primary factor are central, orthogonal, sum to one — the proof of §1 does not assume two components); what is unverified is the per-block *value* law in the `r > 2` case.

**The carne that would test it.** The smallest virgin `>2`-component cell that is RAM-reachable on 8 GB is `(4,14)` char 2 split into its true three components (the `(t−1)` part and the two cubic factors of `t^7 − 1` over `F_2`). The dump engine [`engines/CHUCHIPACHI_v2.cpp`](engines/CHUCHIPACHI_v2.cpp) groups the two cubics into a single `B` block (running the cell as two-component), so the existing `(4,14)` run does **not** test the r-ladder; an engine that splits `B` into its two cubic blocks (a `3^5 = 243`-block lattice) is required. This is logged as the next measured target.

## 5. Honest scope — what this is and is not

- **Proven:** torsion localizes to a single CRT block, for every cell (§1).
- **Strongly validated, out of sample, with one open pen-and-paper residual:** the per-block rank is a product of two recursion ladders, each a reduced-dimension DS value (§2). The residual is to prove the CRT cut equals the DS §17 coordinate cut.
- **Verified byte-exact:** the sofa block-count law (§3).
- **Conjectural, untested:** the r-ladder generalization to `>2`-component cells (§4).

**The detector status.** Localization (§1) is the *where*; the validated law (§2) is the *what*. A certified torsion detector needs both, and needs §2's residual proven — because a law fit to primitive cells predicts "primitive" by construction, so only a *proof* of the value law makes a measured departure mean torsion rather than a gap in the law. Until then, and per the binding requirement in the main README, no nonzero scar would be trusted as a real torsion verdict without a synthetic positive control firing correctly first.

**The graveyard (dead veins, kept so they are not retried).**
- *The partition↔mask dictionary.* An early plan to certify the law by mapping DS's coordinate-partitions `J` (count `(2d+1)!!` — 15 for `n=4`, 105 for `n=6`) to our CRT masks (count `2^NV` — 32 for `n=4`, 128 for `n=6`) piece-by-piece was **refuted with data**: the counts misalign (`15 ≠ 32`, `105 ≠ 128`) and our linear layer has no DS counterpart. The dictionary does not exist as a piecewise correspondence; the correct road to certification is the centrality proof of §1, which needs no dictionary.
- *The "total = DS" check is vacuous as evidence.* Summing the product law over all masks reproduces `DS_n(m)` for *any* split `dimA + dimB = m − 1`, including non-existent splits — it is a binomial identity true by construction. It therefore proves nothing about per-block correctness. It is a necessary **consistency check** (it reflects the characteristic-independence of `dim_C`: different primes give different splits, all of which must sum to the same `dim_C`, so a law whose total depended on the split would be wrong) — but it is not evidence for the block structure. The real evidence is the per-block match and the blind prediction (§2).

**What this is not.** This concerns the **integral** Hodge conjecture for **Fermat** varieties. It is not the Clay Millennium (rational) Hodge conjecture and makes no claim on it. The swan has not been found; every measured block is primitive. What the decomposition adds is an explicit recursive anatomy of these cells' cohomology, a *proof* that any torsion is confined to one computable block, and a from-the-sofa map of where to look and what number would betray a counterexample — the per-block elementary-divisor structure that Aljovin–Movasati–Villaflor explicitly left uncomputed.
